# Opportunity Research — Semiconductor Supplier / Quality OS (Hypothesis C)

**Status:** RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS — not customer-validated. Labeled hypothesis only.
**Canonical context:** `AGENTOS_OPERATING_PROMPT.md` §21; `AGENTOS_STATE.md` §2.
**Date:** 8 September 2026

## Framing
Explore whether a supplier-quality operating system for semiconductor supply chains is a stronger opportunity than hypothesis A. Keep separate until interviews decide.

## Structure
- Problem: semiconductor suppliers and their customers struggle with quality data fragmentation, disposition decisions, and corrective actions; AI agents could help analyze and act but need controlled, auditable execution.
- Customer: supplier quality engineering leaders, commodity managers, quality VPs at chipmakers/suppliers and OEMs.
- User: quality engineers, supplier engineers, reliability engineers.
- Workflow: incoming/material quality review, disposition (accept/reject), 8D/CAPA, spec-change approvals, supplier scorecards.
- Current alternatives: QMS platforms, spreadsheets, email disposition chains, portals.
- Pain hypotheses: slow, error-prone disposition; no governance trail for AI-assisted decisions; audit exposure; supplier disputes.
- Frequency/severity: disposition is high-frequency; error severity high (yield/cost).
- Economic value: faster, correct dispositions; reduced escapes; auditability.
- Data requirements: lot/spec/test data; secure data sharing across org boundaries (sensitive).
- AI advantage: anomaly detection and disposition recommendation; human approval with evidence.
- Moat: data + workflow + cross-company trust.
- Distribution: QMS vendors, supply-chain platforms, semiconductor ecosystems.
- Competitive landscape: QMS/quality suites, analytics startups.
- Validation questions: how are dispositions decided today? What is the cost of a bad disposition/escape? Would you let an AI recommend with human sign-off under full audit? What integration is mandatory?

No customer evidence exists yet. Do not treat interest as validation.
